# Sidebars

Short, self-contained labs on things this course assumes you already know.

**These are almost certainly not going to be covered in class.** They are here
because a course has to assume something, and what it assumes is never the
same as what any particular person walked in with.

Take them at a break, over lunch, after class, next week, or never. Nothing later in
the course depends on your having done one. Nothing is tracked. Nobody will ask.

### Who these are for

Anyone whose knowledge of a thing has gone stale, which is everyone, about something.

Eight years in a codebase that never turned on nullable reference types is not a gap
in your ability — it is a gap in your exposure, and it closes in about fifteen
minutes. The same is true of records if you were writing C# before they existed, or
of environment variables if every environment you have ever worked in was handed to
you already configured.

Pick the ones that look unfamiliar. Skip the rest. Most people will open two.

### How they are written

Each one starts with what it is for, so you can decide from the first paragraph
whether to spend the fifteen minutes. Then steps. Answers are in the lab, collapsed —
click to reveal when you want them, and there is no virtue in not clicking.

They are recipes. They tell you how, not why. The why is what the course is for.

Each one ends with three prompts you can paste into an AI assistant to go further than
two pages allow. They are written to be worth asking — one of them will always produce
something you can run and check for yourself, which matters, because an explanation of
something you do not know is not something you can verify.

### If something here is not here

Ask. Put it in the parking lot in Hall Monitor and it will get answered, and if it
turns out three classes in a row wanted the same thing, it becomes one of these.
